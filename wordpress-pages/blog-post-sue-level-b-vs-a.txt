<p style="font-size:1.125rem">If you're a contractor, engineer, or project manager working on Virginia infrastructure, you've probably heard the terms <strong>SUE Level B</strong> and <strong>SUE Level A</strong> thrown around. But what's actually different between them — and when does your project require each level?</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">What is SUE? A Quick Primer</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Subsurface Utility Engineering (SUE)</strong> is a branch of engineering that manages the risks associated with subsurface utility information. It's governed by <strong>ASCE Standard 38-02</strong>, which defines four quality levels (A through D) for how accurately underground utilities are located and documented.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The higher the level (A being highest), the more accurate and legally defensible the data. Each level requires progressively more invasive — and more reliable — methods.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">The Four SUE Quality Levels</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:table -->
<figure class="wp-block-table"><table><thead><tr><th>Quality Level</th><th>Method</th><th>Accuracy</th><th>Best For</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td><strong>Level D</strong></td><td>Records &amp; maps only</td><td>Lowest — often wrong</td><td>Early planning only</td></tr><tr><td><strong>Level C</strong></td><td>Field surveys of visible features</td><td>Surface indicators only</td><td>Preliminary design</td></tr><tr><td><strong>Level B</strong></td><td>Geophysical (GPR, EM, etc.)</td><td>Horizontal location ±3 feet typical</td><td>Design, planning, bid documents</td></tr><tr><td><strong>Level A</strong></td><td>Physical exposure (hydro excavation)</td><td>Exact — verified X, Y, Z coordinates</td><td>Final design, construction, legal</td></tr></tbody></table></figure>
<!-- /wp:table -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">SUE Level B: Geophysical Detection</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Level B uses non-invasive geophysical equipment — ground-penetrating radar (GPR), electromagnetic induction (EM), or acoustic tools — to detect buried utilities from the surface. The result is a map showing <em>approximate</em> horizontal location.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">What Level B Gives You</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Horizontal position of utilities (±1–3 feet accuracy depending on soil and depth)</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->
<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Utility type identification in many cases</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->
<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Mapping suitable for design drawings and bid documents</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->
<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>No physical excavation required</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">What Level B Cannot Tell You</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Exact depth of buried utilities</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->
<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Condition, material, or size of the utility</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->
<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Whether a utility has been abandoned or rerouted</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->
<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Precise vertical location for conflict analysis</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">SUE Level A: Physical Exposure (Hydro Excavation)</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Level A is the gold standard. It requires physically exposing the utility — and <strong>hydro excavation (potholing/daylighting)</strong> is the accepted method. A technician uses high-pressure water and vacuum to safely expose the utility, then a surveyor records its exact X, Y, and Z (depth) coordinates.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">What Level A Gives You</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Exact horizontal and vertical position — surveyed to sub-inch accuracy</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->
<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Physical confirmation of utility type, size, material, and condition</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->
<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Legally defensible documentation for engineering records</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->
<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Required data for VDOT SUE submittals on qualifying projects</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->
<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Conflict analysis confidence — you know exactly where the utility is in 3D space</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Why Hydro Excavation is Required for Level A</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>ASCE 38 specifies that Level A data must come from <em>physical exposure</em>. Mechanical excavation (backhoes, hand digging) risks damaging the very utilities you're trying to locate. Hydro excavation — using pressurized water and vacuum — safely exposes utilities without contact damage, making it the only practical method for achieving Level A verification at scale.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">When Do You Need Level B vs Level A?</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Use Level B When:</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>You're in early design and need approximate utility locations for planning</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->
<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>The project area is large and budget doesn't support full Level A coverage</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->
<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>You need to identify which specific locations require Level A follow-up</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->
<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>The contract or agency only requires Level B for design submission</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Use Level A When:</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>VDOT or other agencies require it for the project scope</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->
<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Mechanical excavation is planned near existing utilities</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->
<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>The design has utility conflicts that must be resolved before construction</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->
<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>You need legally defensible documentation for liability protection</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->
<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>A utility owner requires exposure before granting construction clearance</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Cost Comparison: Level B vs Level A</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Level B is significantly cheaper per location — geophysical scanning costs a fraction of physical exposure. However, Level A provides data that Level B cannot, and the cost of a utility strike ($50,000–$500,000+ in repairs, delays, and liability) dwarfs the cost of proper Level A verification.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
