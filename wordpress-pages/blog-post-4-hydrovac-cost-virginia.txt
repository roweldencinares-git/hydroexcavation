<h2 class="wp-block-heading">What Drives Hydro Excavation Cost?</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">1. Mobilization Distance</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Most contractors charge a mobilization fee based on distance from their yard. A Virginia Beach-based operator like Beach HydroVac typically reaches Norfolk, Chesapeake, Suffolk, and Newport News without significant travel premiums.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">2. Depth of Excavation</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Shallow utilities (2–4 feet) are faster to expose than deep ones (8–12 feet). Deeper potholes require more time, water pressure, and vacuum cycles — increasing cost per hole.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">3. Soil Type</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Virginia Beach's sandy coastal soils are generally easier (and faster) to hydrovac than the clay-heavy soils you'll find inland. Faster excavation = lower cost. Hampton Roads' soil conditions often work in the contractor's favor.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">4. Access to the Site</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>If your truck can park close to the work area, it's fast and efficient. Restricted access sites (alleys, residential lots, fenced utility corridors) may require extended hose setups — which is where Beach HydroVac's <strong>600-foot hose reach</strong> becomes a significant cost advantage over competitors with shorter reach.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">5. Volume of Work</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>A single pothole costs more per unit than a 20-hole potholing program. Day rates and volume pricing usually make larger projects significantly more cost-efficient. If you have multiple locations, discuss a project rate.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">6. Documentation Requirements</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Standard potholing (just exposing the utility) costs less than SUE Level A verification, which requires professional survey coordination, measurements, and documentation delivered in CAD-ready format. If your project requires ASCE-compliant data, budget accordingly — but remember the $4–$21 cost savings per dollar spent that SUE Level A typically delivers.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
